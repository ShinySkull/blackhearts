Player Backstory

Peck

“Your true name, boy, that must always be a secret. That’s why we all have our names.”

At the Table

Who he is: Nicolaus, known only as Peck, a halfling raised in Goldy’s Cathouse on the Chalmouth docks. He grew up running errands for the Tall Hats, a secret crime family, and became their stalker, enforcer and killer. He is a sworn associate, not yet “made”, and he wants that more than anything. He was pinched in a raid on the Salt Exchange, tortured in Blackwater Prison, and escaped in chains into the Gloombough.

Strengths: Small, brave and easy to overlook. He can slip through a crowded tavern, strike, and be gone. He speaks thieves’ cant, can spot a lie or a fat purse, and knows the streets, the precautions and the dangers. He fights with blade and fist, strikes first, and always confirms the kill. He can sail and knows the sea from his father. He kept his mouth shut under torture.

Weaknesses and flaws: He lives for image, respect and fine things, and his greed and bloodlust have grown with the life. He believes there is no honor in war and will strike an unaware enemy without hesitation. He has no life outside the family, and will drop anything when his uncle calls. He arrives with nothing: no blade, no knuckle duster, no clothes but rags, and manacles still on his wrists.

Morality and religion: The family is his religion. Loyalty, silence and respect are the only laws he keeps: never admit the family exists, never rat, and always come when called. He has killed the innocent, authorities and women on orders, but he draws the line at children.

Why the Blackhearts: He escaped Blackwater with the authorities’ hounds on his heels, and the Gloombough was the only place they wouldn’t follow. He needs somewhere safe to shake the heat before he can go home. He still carries Goldy’s coded message for One Eye, and he means to deliver it.

Hooks for the DM:

  • The message for One Eye, still in Peck’s head and still undelivered. What did it say, and what happened in Chalmouth because it never arrived?
  • The raid on the Salt Exchange. The Admiralty knew his name and knew he was carrying a message. Who tipped them off, and who are the “agents” they meant to place within the family?
  • Goldy, a gnome who is somehow Peck’s uncle, and who kept sending Finnius out to sea. What is the real tie between them, and does the family believe Peck kept quiet in Blackwater?
  • Finnius, lost to pirates on the way to Triventium. Was he really lost?
  • Magistrate Corvin Pell and the Admiralty, who lost a prisoner out of Blackwater and know exactly who he is.
  • His mother, the woman Goldy dismissed in one cruel sentence.

Inception

Nicolaus began his life in Chalmouth, close to the docks along the Greywater. His family consisted of only his father, Finnius, who was a sailor and smuggler that worked for his uncle, Goldy. His mother he never knew. His uncle always said, “She was just some whore your father liked.” Nicolaus never understood how Goldy was his uncle, as he was a gnome and Nicolaus’s family were halflings, but his father assured him that it was so. Initially he was too naive to know. While his father was out at sea he stayed with his uncle, who ran a brothel and gambling house known as Goldy’s Cathouse.

It was at Goldy’s that Nicolaus was molded by his uncle to learn what respect and family truly meant. Though his father did teach him to sail, and the use of a knife like any good sailor, his father was always at sea. His uncle sent Finnius away often, and Finnius was always reluctant to leave Nicolaus with his uncle, but he did as he was told. The cathouse is where Nicolaus observed and learned his family’s true trade: power. He accompanied his uncle as a boy, often used to hold illegal items or posted as a lookout. He learned soon that many in his uncle’s company had strange names: Crazy Hugh, Fat Lip, Crooked Ear, Neckbone, Bill the Blade. Even his uncle, who everyone knew as Goldy. He asked his uncle one day why this was so, and that was the day he received his name.

“Your true name, boy, that must always be a secret. That’s why we all have our names, nephew. And today, you shall receive yours. You can’t pick it. It must be chosen for you.”

“Whaddabout Peck? Haha, he is smaller than you, Goldy,” said Neckbone.

“Aye, a small wun he is now, eh boss?” mumbled Bill the Blade.

Goldy pondered his subordinates’ suggestions, took a deep drag of his cigar, and laughed, smoke spilling from his mouth in puffs.

“Aye lads, he is smaller. But the boy has heart, I can see it in his eyes, like his father. Peck! Peck is what your name is now, boy.” He leaned close into Peck’s face, smoke billowing from his cigar. He took it out, placed a hand on Peck’s shoulder, and said:

“This is our thing, nephew. Let me teach you about what our thing is.”

Initiation

As the years passed, the innocence of Peck’s youth was quickly refined into a cold, calculated and respected man. As his father was frequently away at sea conducting business for Goldy, Peck was always at the cathouse and always with Goldy. As he grew older, he learned more skills. How to move in the streets, precautions to take, dangers to be aware of. He learned the secret language of thieves, the phrases and the slang associated with them. He learned how to spot a lie, and he learned how to spot someone with money. He learned how to make money, and most importantly, he learned what respect and power truly meant.

He also learned about violence, and how to use it as a message.

He learned the blade from his uncle and other family members, and the fist and how to use it as well. Peck was small, but he was brave, and his uncle knew this was one of his best qualities. His uncle would soon deploy him as an asset.

Goldy needed someone stalked? Peck would be the choice. Goldy needed someone sent a message? Peck is who he would send. Goldy needed someone to have their head cracked? Peck would get it done for his uncle. Peck became infatuated with the action of the life, from collecting debts, to hitting scores, to murder. He relished the respect he got from his peers, the respect of walking into an establishment and being known and respected by all instantly. Even feared.

When he was still a youth, about to become a man, his uncle brought him into his office to talk. His father, Finnius, had not returned from a long voyage to Triventium, and Peck had wondered why there had been no word. Goldy bid him come in and sit down. He puffed his cigar and said:

“Listen, kid, your dad, he’s not coming back. They are lost, attacked by pirates before they could make port. I know your dad was always at sea, but he trusted me to keep you safe, to watch out for you. It’s time you know something. This thing of ours, our thing, it’s a family, as you know. But now that your father is gone, I am your sole guardian, your uncle.”

“You’ve proven that you are dedicated to this family. And loyalty is everything in this game, kid. You’ve got it in spades.”

“You already know to keep your mouth shut. I’ve been given permission to induct you as an official associate of our family, the Tall Hats. You are never, ever to admit its existence, or speak about our family in any way to an outsider. Ever.”

Goldy puffed his cigar and leaned closer to Peck. “You will dedicate your life to the service of this family, at any hour, on any day, for any reason. Your wife gets sick and your uncle calls you, YOU COME. Everything from this day forward is second to your family.”

Putting in Work

Significant time had passed since Goldy had inducted Peck into our thing as an associate. Peck was always busy, but he loved the life he lived. He couldn’t get enough of it. Peck was now carrying out missions solo, or with a crew. Like a wraith, he could enter a crowded tavern, find his mark, and in the chaos of revelry and drunkenness, he would strike. A swift blow to the armpit with a dagger, or to the groin, or poison poured while everyone cheered. Like a ghost had struck. Peck was versatile in the way he stalked and hunted his mark.

Peck’s heart had grown cold and calloused by this time. There was no job he would not take for his family. Interrogations, beating or cutting the information out while his uncle asked questions. Murders: the innocent, those in the “game”, authorities, even women, though Peck drew the line at children. Thankfully that was never asked of him.

Peck was in good standing with his uncle and his family, but he was still an associate. He was not a “made man” yet, though he strove to be. He continued to operate with professionalism, just as his uncle wanted. He struck first against his enemies, as he quickly learned in this life there is no “honor” in war. You strike when your enemy is unaware. You take advantage of every opportunity to make the killing blow before your prey knows you are there. You strike with cold calculation, the arteries, the heart, and you always confirm your kill with a dagger driven under the throat and into the skull and brain.

As his bloodlust grew with the adrenaline and the thrill of the crime, his greed also grew. He became corrupted by the opulence and finery of his family’s lifestyle. The best clothes, the finest food and women. His image and respect were all that mattered to him, because that was all he had.

Pinched

Peck continued to thrive within his family. He was in good standing and had garnered respect not only from his own family, but from other extended members as well. His uncle told him it would only be a matter of time before he would petition for him to be “made”. This was what Peck truly wanted, but he knew that these things would take time. His uncle continued to reassure him.

One day he was summoned by Goldy and given instruction to take a coded message to the underboss. Peck already knew that any message carried to another in the family was always committed to memory, and always in code. It was to be delivered personally to the ear of whoever was to hear it. His uncle whispered him the code, and said:

“Take this to your uncle One Eye. He will be expecting you. Make haste, this message is of critical importance.”

Peck nodded and made his way. He didn’t run, but he moved with purpose. As he was entering the Salt Exchange, he saw something suspicious from the corner of his eye. Two figures darted past the alleyway on the street opposite him. Then he saw another thing that was off: merchants wearing bulky clothing and looking around nervously. It was summer, too hot for that. He went to turn right, and a wagon pulled across and blocked the way. Sensing danger, he quickened his pace before he heard:

“Nobody move! This is a raid! No exit until everyone here is thoroughly searched, by order of Magistrate Corvin Pell!”

Shit, a raid, he thought to himself. Trying to blend in, he was spotted by a federal officer of the Admiralty. “HIM, that one there! Arrest him immediately!” Before Peck could react, he was detained and taken to Blackwater Prison for interrogation.

Escape to Gloombough

Manacles, chains, clubs to the body and head. “Get down! That one here, he knows something! Take him away!” He was picked up and thrown into a wagon with bars, one of only three. Peck didn’t know the others in the wagon, but they all knew where they were going, and what would happen there. Blackwater: torture, despair, death. The wagon rolled its way to the dungeon.

Peck was separated from the others in the wagon and taken to an interrogation room. Put to work. He could smell the wine on the breath of his tormentors. Perhaps they needed it to steel themselves for such work?

“Are you the one known as Peck?”

“What do you know about the Tall Hats?”

“We know you received a message to carry. What was it?”

“We’ve heard of a family, our thing. We have agents to place you within their structure.”

“Give us a name, or we will hurt you.”

Peck steeled himself, grimly raised his head, looked into the eyes of his interrogator, and said:

“I don’t know anything. I want a lawyer.”

His lawyer never came, or was even called. It was fists, brands, and blades that made their mark on him, seeking to make him release his information. He held fast. He stuck to the code. His respect and honor meant more to him than anything. He wouldn’t rat. But he knew he wouldn’t last under continued interrogation.

They took him to a small holding cell within the interrogation chamber. A single cell, small and cramped, with a single window facing the Greywater. They had taken everything from Peck. His clothes were torn, his blade gone, his knuckle duster gone, all his possessions. He looked about his cell and saw the small window. It was barred for a human-sized person, but not for a halfling. He climbed up and saw that the bars were degraded from the spray of the sea. Salt. His father always talked about how it ruined things, as a sailor. He knew he could wiggle through.

And wiggle through he did, yet he was still bound in manacled chains, two cuffs and a link of chain between the wrists. Luckily they were secured in the front. He peered down at the bay: a long fall that must be timed between waves. He prepared himself as he heard the jingle of keys coming up the stairwell to his chamber and his cell. He did not have time. Wiggling out and finding a footing, he timed the waves amongst the rocks. As he prepared to leap with the waves, the door to his cell opened. “HEY! HE’S ESCAPING!” As Peck leaped into the waters below, he heard the cry of “Alaaaaarm!”

Splash into the water, limbs thankfully spared from the rocks. He swam. And swam. And swam. Reaching the shore, he knew he had only one hope: the Gloombough. It was a far distance, but he knew that when outrunning the authorities, you ran until you couldn’t run. And then you ran some more. He could hear the alarm being raised, the hounds barking and the shouts of men and horses. He ran. He hid in the mud and went through streams and rivers, all while the authorities tracked and hunted him for days.

Finally, the Gloombough was in the distance as the hounds bayed behind him. “Stop! He’s going to the Gloombough, call off the hounds! It’s a damn nest of thieves in there. The little bastard will probably freeze to death, or starve. He’s chained, he’s helpless. Call off the search, we return home.”

Peck knew his only sanctuary was the Gloombough, and the Blackhearts. He would carry his message back to his family, but he needed to shake the heat before he could. At least there, it was safe. Better than a prison.
