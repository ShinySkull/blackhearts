Player Backstory

Bowyer Campbell

“They taught me that an oath is a thing you die for. They were wrong. An oath is a thing you get killed for.”

The Name

House Campbell was an old name in Kelvothis and a poor one. It held a manor in the upland valleys west of the city, a seat in the Citadel’s lesser halls, and debts at the Goldweigh Exchange that went back three generations. When Lord Ewan Campbell died of a fever in Bowyer’s eleventh winter, the merchants who held those debts took the land within the month. They took the horses, the plate, and the hall. They left the boy his name, since a name was the only thing they couldn’t sell.

Most sons of a fallen house end up in a counting-house or a gutter. Bowyer was spared both. His father had once served House Ardane, a greater family of the Nine Towers, and Lord Merrick Ardane honored the old debt by taking the boy into his household to be trained as a housecarl. That meant a sworn sword, a shield-bearer, a man who stands between his lord and a knife.

Bowyer was good at it. He learned the long sword and the round shield, how to stand a post for eight hours without shifting his weight, and how to watch a room full of nobles and know which one was lying. He learned that a housecarl has no wants of his own. He was told the reward was honor, and at fifteen he believed it.

The Fall

At seventeen he was one of two boys being considered as sworn shield to the Ardane heir. The other was Osric Thorne, a cousin of a cousin, handsome and well-liked.

One morning a casket of Ardane silver went missing from the household vaults. It was found that afternoon under the floorboards beneath Bowyer’s cot, with the vault key from his own belt sitting on top of it.

He never confessed, because he had nothing to confess. It made no difference. Lord Merrick spared his life out of respect for his father’s memory, and that was the only mercy he got. Bowyer was flogged in the stable yard in front of the whole household. Then his sword was broken over a mounting block and he was put out the postern gate at dusk with the clothes on his back. Osric Thorne watched from the gallery and didn’t look away. A month later Osric was sworn shield to the heir.

Bowyer learned two things that day. Innocence is worth nothing. And the people who swear oaths at you are the ones holding the knife.

The Gutter

The lower city of Kelvothis sits in the shadow of the Nine Towers, and the Old Gallows stands at the center of it. Bowyer slept beneath it for most of a winter. He stole bread, then purses. He was beaten by older boys until he learned to hit back first, and to hit where it would end the fight. A housecarl’s training turned out to be just as good for that.

At nineteen he was picked up by a crew called the Vault Rats, smugglers and fences who worked out of the abandoned wine vaults under the merchant quarter. They didn’t want him for his skill. They wanted him because he had a noble’s bearing and nobody who would miss him. He was a disposable man for disposable jobs. They gave him a knife and a name, a moneylender in arrears, and they fully expected the watch to take him afterward.

The watch didn’t take him.

He found the moneylender in an alley behind the Lantern Steps, and the man begged. Bowyer found that he felt nothing about the begging, nothing at all. Then the knife went in, and for the first time since the stable yard something in him went quiet and still and clear. He felt like he was standing his post again. Everything that had been taken from him seemed to come back for the length of one held breath.

He walked back to the vaults with blood drying on his cuffs, and he was smiling.

The Knife

The Vault Rats stopped treating him as disposable. Over the next four years Bowyer Campbell became the crew’s knife. He worked the lower city, then the Valroad west to Tarnford, where the Rats had business with the cattle drovers and a quiet arrangement at Pike’s Tannery. A body put into a tanning pit at night was nothing but leather and lime by spring.

Bowyer was patient, precise, and courteous. He spoke to his targets the way a housecarl speaks to a lord, and some of them thanked him right up until they understood. He didn’t drink or gamble, and he didn’t keep company. What he kept was the quiet he found in the work, and he wanted more of it than the contracts could give him.

So he started taking it without contracts. A drover here, a traveler on the Valroad there, people nobody would trace back to the Rats. Then people started noticing. Bailiff Joren Pike began asking questions in Tarnford about missing men, and about tanning pits that held more than hides.

The Vault Rats did what powerful people do with a problem. They arranged to give the bailiff a name, and the name they chose was Bowyer’s.

The Second Fall

He saw it coming this time. He had seen this once before, in the gallery above a stable yard.

The Rats met in the back room of the Ox and Cleaver to agree on the price. Bowyer was already in the room, and the meeting did not go the way they planned. The bailiff’s men found five bodies the next morning, set out in a neat row with their hands folded, as if laid out for a funeral. There was a sixth Vault Rat, a young fence called Wick, and he wasn’t among them.

Wick had lived long enough to bargain. He had nothing to offer but a secret: the Rats had sometimes moved goods for someone bigger, someone who lived out in the Gloombough. There was a trail east of the lake that ran past the ruined sanctuary at Moth Hollow, and a black heart was painted on a crypt door there. You left a message under the heart and they came to you.

Bowyer thanked him with real courtesy. Then he laid him out next to the others.

The Blackhearts

There is a price on Bowyer Campbell in Tarnford now, and probably in Kelvothis as well. There is no house that will take him, no oath he believes in, and no lord he will ever kneel to again. That suits him.

He doesn’t come to the Blackhearts for brotherhood or for coin, although he’ll take the coin. He comes because they are the only people in the borderlands whose whole business is the thing he loves. He comes because a brotherhood of murderers will never ask him to stop. And the Blackhearts sell death to the highest bidder, so sooner or later one of those bidders will be a lord of the Nine Towers. Some lords in Kelvothis have sworn shields who’ll stand between them and a knife.

He waited three nights at Moth Hollow among the crypts and the pale moths, and he wasn’t afraid. On the fourth night someone came.

At the Table

Who he is: The disgraced son of a dead minor lord of Kelvothis. He trained as a housecarl, was framed and cast out, and became a gutter thief, then a gang’s hired knife, and finally a serial killer. He is neutral evil, calm and polite, and completely numb to everything except the kill.

Strengths: Housecarl discipline: he can hold a post, hold his nerve, and read a room. He has noble manners and bearing and can pass in high society. He is patient, methodical, and leaves no witnesses.

Weaknesses and flaws: He needs to kill, and the longer he goes without it, the more careless he gets. He cannot trust anyone, because everyone who has sworn loyalty to him has betrayed him. Under the numbness he is still proud of the Campbell name and will not use another.

Morality and religion: He sees oaths, honor, and law as chains the strong put on the weak. He prayed in the Citadel chapels as a boy and nothing answered. The only true and honest thing he has ever found is the moment of death, and he treats it with an almost religious reverence.

Why the Blackhearts: He is a hunted man with nowhere left to go. The Blackhearts are the one place where his appetite is an asset. And a contract into Kelvothis could one day put him in a room with House Ardane and Osric Thorne.

Hooks for the DM: - Osric Thorne, now sworn shield to the Ardane heir. Did he plant the silver, and was someone else behind it? - House Ardane, which still holds the evidence of Bowyer’s “crime” and has some reason to want him to stay dead. - Bailiff Joren Pike, who has five bodies laid out in a row and one name to put to them. - The Goldweigh Exchange creditors who took the Campbell lands. Who actually holds that debt today?
